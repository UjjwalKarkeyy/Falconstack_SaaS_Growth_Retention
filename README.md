Revenue growth is not yet sustainably healthy. MRR and customer acquisition grew strongly through most of 2024, but retention deteriorated sharply in late 2024. Active customers fell from 227 in August to 142 in December, while churn rose from 16.48% in November to 36.82% in December. NRR also remained below 100% in October and November, showing that expansion was not fully offsetting churn and contraction.

Churn appears to be driven by a mix of feature gaps, budget pressure, support issues, competition, and pricing rather than one single cause. Higher-risk segments include DevTools, Event-sourced customers, trial accounts, and Enterprise accounts.

Product usage is associated with better retention, but only moderately. Retained customers show slightly higher usage and broader feature adoption, suggesting that engagement helps, but product quality and friction still matter.

The strongest investment candidates are Organic and Partner acquisition channels, Cybersecurity, EdTech, and FinTech, where revenue contribution is relatively strong and churn is more manageable.

Core takeaway: acquisition is working, but retention became the main constraint on growth in late 2024. The priority should shift from simply acquiring more customers to improving early retention, product adoption, support quality, and the durability of high-value customer segments.
